# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Video editing clients — creative producers, marketing teams, agencies, and direct clients looking for an editor with technical range. They're evaluating craft, not resumes. They want proof of work across genres (tech tutorials, creative shorts, long-form) and confidence the editor can handle their specific project.

## Product Purpose

Showcase Rogerio Barbosa's video editing portfolio. Demonstrate technical skill, creative range, and professional polish across every project. Convert visitors into leads via the contact form. The site itself is the portfolio's best testimony: if the editor's own site isn't well-edited, nothing else matters.

## Brand Personality

Dark, bold, energetic. Confident like a midnight film title — not loud, but powerful. Not minimalist — intentional. Every element earns its place. The tone is professional with an edge: this is a craftsman who knows both DaVinci Resolve and React.

## Anti-references

- The saturated AI-modern template look: cream/sand/beige backgrounds, glassmorphism, geometric decorative shapes, gradient text, numbered "01 / 02 / 03" section markers on every section, tiny all-caps tracked "ABOUT / PROCESS / PRICING" kickers above every heading.
- Old-school video editor clichés: film reel clip art, clapperboard icons, cheesy wipe transitions, "video production" stock imagery.
- Generic SaaS landing pages with hero-metric templates (big number + small label + gradient accent).
- Identical card grids with icon + heading + text repeated endlessly.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** Polish, pacing, and craft are the portfolio's own testimony. If it's a video editor's site, it must feel edited — every transition, every spacing choice, every interaction.

2. **Show, don't tell.** Work samples and visual proof outweigh any claim. Thumbnails, clips, and case studies carry the argument. Typography and layout create the frame; the work is the center.

3. **Confident restraint.** Bold doesn't mean busy. Dark backgrounds, dramatic moments, with breathing room. Let the purple/cyan/pink accent palette hit at the right moments, not everywhere. Rarity is the point.

4. **Genre-switch fluency.** The same editor handles tech tutorials and creative shorts. The site must flex across that range — tech content gets cyan/clean framing, creative work gets pink/bolder framing — without feeling like two different sites.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA best-effort target (4.5:1 body contrast, keyboard-navigable, reduced motion support). Given the dark theme, ensure text-on-surface contrast is verified. Skip animations when `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` is set.
